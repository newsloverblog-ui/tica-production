# Tica platform rebuild — implementation audit

Source of truth: `tica.ng Product & Engineering Brief: Platform Rebuild Priorities` (30 October 2026).

Audit date: 9 November 2026  
Codebase: Laravel 13 / PHP 8.3–8.5 application in this workspace  
Legend: **Done** = implemented and locally verified; **Partial** = useful foundation exists but does not meet the brief; **Blocked** = requires credentials, infrastructure, data migration, policy, or product decisions unavailable in this workspace.

## Executive summary

The repository is a mature ticketing application, not a greenfield rebuild. It already has events, ticket types, guest checkout, coupons, multiple gateways, organizer withdrawals, invoice generation, QR scanning, reports, CSV exports and a corporate-registration MVP. The largest remaining risks are production operations, incomplete live/staging validation, legacy payment and ticket migration, privacy/accessibility sign-off, and the advanced corporate-registration modules that sit beyond the MVP.

This pass completed the safe, high-confidence Phase 0 work that can be implemented without production credentials or business-policy decisions:

- non-empty default SEO metadata on all frontend pages;
- complete Open Graph, Twitter card, canonical URL, and event structured data output;
- stable canonical event slug redirects;
- current-year footer placeholder repair (`{year}` and legacy `{2025}`);
- consistent formatted prices and removal of literal multi-date asterisks;
- years added to public event-card dates;
- truthful checkout assurance cues;
- signed ticket payloads for newly generated QR codes;
- authenticated, CSRF-protected, organizer-scoped, atomic check-in;
- queued, private, filter-aware CSV/XLSX/PDF booking exports for organizers and admins, with durable history, actor-scoped signed downloads and retention cleanup;
- durable export-completion notices for administrators, organizers, customers and scanner supervisors, with one encrypted mail ledger per export, actor-owned expiring links and minutely recovery;
- privacy-safe first-party funnel analytics for search, event view, ticket selection, checkout, authoritative payment outcomes, ticket delivery and check-in, with an authorized admin conversion view.
- durable, observable queue-handoff recovery for paid event confirmations and paid product-order confirmations, with encrypted scheduling errors, unique jobs, minute-level redispatch and stale incidents that remain separate from provider-delivery attempts; subscriber and push campaign parents also return to recoverable pending state if recipient fan-out exhausts.

## Phase 1 — payments, tickets, check-in, registration, exports

### Payments and inventory

Active Paystack and Flutterwave event fulfilment, corporate invoice payment and the contained legacy Iyzico verifier now share one locked event-sale accounting boundary. The gateway/reference ledger identity is the idempotency boundary; organizer credit, platform revenue and the sale ledger commit atomically. Replays must match booking, gross amount, tax, commission, customer, organizer, payment method and paid status or the entire transition fails closed.

Platform earnings are also a database-enforced singleton rather than a convention. Every authoritative event/product sale, manual decision, refund and cancellation reversal locks the same row through a shared service; a missing or ambiguous row aborts the surrounding transaction. The migration creates the zero row only for an empty installation, adds the unique singleton constraint, and refuses to guess how duplicate historical balances should be combined. Release readiness validates the live row as well as the schema, so a later privileged deletion or malformed singleton blocks traffic. Production deployment must reconcile any duplicate history to one finance-approved row before retrying the migration.

### Payouts and finance

Organizer KYC now has private ID/business-document storage, admin review, explicit unverified/pending/verified/rejected states, audited decisions and a truthful verified badge. Verified organizers can add a Nigerian bank account through Paystack account resolution; the resolved owner name must match the verified legal name, full account numbers are encrypted, duplicate destinations are blocked, and every new withdrawal is locked to the verified destination. The finance queue now supports pending, hold, paid and rejected states, requires a unique external payout reference before marking paid, restores rejected balances atomically, and audits every decision. Organizer single/bulk cancellation, finance hold/paid/rejected decisions and Paystack payout release share one locked evidence reconciler: exactly one withdrawal transaction must match owner, method, amount, reservation and lifecycle status. Missing, duplicate, cross-owner or mismatched evidence fails closed; terminal replay verifies rather than moving the balance again, while provider confirmation with lost evidence becomes an audited and alerted `needs_attention` transfer. Scheduled payout verification is isolated and bounded to the oldest 1–500 due transfers; a failed lookup advances behind untouched work, keeps detailed provider text only in the encrypted transfer diagnostic and writes only exception type/code metadata to logs, audit records and incidents.

Organizer finance statements now report gross sales, platform fees, refunds, manual adjustments, current available balance, pending/held payouts, paid payouts and payout fees for a selected date range, with immediate CSV/XLSX/PDF exports and audited access. Large CSV/XLSX statements are counted before materialization, snapshotted at an immutable transaction boundary, generated by an encrypted idempotent background job, privately retained, actor-owned and delivered through the existing expiring signed-download history. Oversized PDF rendering fails closed with guidance to use a streamable format. The interactive finance page computes summary totals in the database rather than loading the entire ledger. Gateway fees remain visibly unallocated rather than being estimated.

The organizer finance page now also exposes the current payout request's operational state. It shows the configured next business-day release window only when the withdrawal passes the same versioned eligibility policy used by automatic release; processing and held requests are explicit, and disabled automation, missing approval, incomplete KYC/bank setup, cooling periods, unresolved disputes/transfers or risk limits show a truthful review/action state without promising a date.

Feature-flagged Paystack recipient creation and transfer initiation are now implemented for verified destinations. Recipient identifiers and gateway responses are encrypted; submission is queue-backed and idempotent; signed transfer webhooks and scheduled verification must match the controlled reference, amount, currency and transfer code before the withdrawal and payout ledger are marked paid. Failed and reversed transfers are held for finance review without automatically returning reserved funds, while network/5xx ambiguity is reconciled and bounded verification exhaustion becomes an audited `needs_attention` case. Release readiness prevents Paystack transfers without the Paystack gateway. Automatic release now has a separate, versioned eligibility engine and remains disabled by default.

Payout outcomes also dispatch encrypted, unique notification jobs with persistent organizer/finance delivery markers. Organizers are told only about confirmed completion or a held terminal outcome; finance receives failed, reversed, indeterminate and verification-attention alerts. Transfer enablement fails release readiness unless `PAYOUT_FINANCE_EMAIL` is valid.

The automatic-release implementation now calculates the next configured business day (including configured holidays), enforces organizer/KYC/bank-account cooling periods, requires at least one successful manual payout by default, bounds single and daily amounts, and blocks held payouts, unresolved transfer outcomes and payout disputes. A dry-run-first `payouts:release-eligible` command reports exact reason codes; apply mode rechecks eligibility under locks, records the policy version and system actor, queues only bounded candidates and runs on the scheduler only when explicitly enabled. Release readiness fails closed unless management approval evidence, a settlement owner and sane policy limits are configured.

Still required before enablement: management must approve the actual limits and settlement ownership and record the non-secret approval reference. A production-like Paystack recipient/transfer/webhook/reconciliation and notification drill is also required. The repository deliberately does not fabricate either approval or provider evidence.

The public pricing page now includes a responsive fee calculator driven by the live fixed per-paid-ticket booking fee and platform commission. Its calculations share the checkout fee service, show ticket sales, attendee booking fees, attendee total, platform fee and organizer proceeds, and explicitly exclude provider processing fees until those are allocated from settlement data. Admin fee configuration is bounded to non-negative fixed fees and a 0-100 percent commission so an unsafe rate cannot be published.

Organizers can now open one controlled dispute at a time against an owned payout, select a bounded issue category and follow finance's response from payout history. Finance sees an oldest-first dispute queue beside the withdrawal controls and can move a case to review, resolve it or reject it with a required closing explanation. Opening and reviewing are audited, ownership is enforced under row locks, the payout amount/status is snapshotted for evidence, and dispute decisions deliberately never move money or rewrite the withdrawal ledger.

- scanner and verification endpoints require organizer authentication;
- CSRF protection is enabled;
- a logged-in organizer can only scan their own event bookings;
- event ID and booking ID must match the signed payload;
- row locking makes duplicate scans atomic across concurrent scanners.
- ticket types now use audited soft retirement instead of hard deletion; multilingual labels and booking history remain available, and an active checkout reservation blocks retirement.

### Entertainment checkout

The public ticket review boundary no longer trusts hidden form pricing or date fields. Online price mode and amounts come from the current ticket record, scalar quantities are bounded by the server-owned purchase limit, authenticated prior admissions reduce the remaining allowance, venue quantity arrays must match the current option catalogue exactly, and event dates are canonicalized or matched against an available event-owned schedule row. A client-side change can therefore no longer downgrade a paid ticket to the free fulfilment path or attach a booking to an arbitrary occurrence.

Guest checkout, ticket types, variations, quantities, coupons and basic availability are present. Checkout is now a progressively enhanced three-step journey (ticket review, attendee details, review/payment) with responsive layout, keyboard/focus handling, reduced-motion support, shared attendee validation and funnel events. Guests proceed only when the administrator setting is enabled; a client-supplied `type=guest` can no longer bypass a disabled policy. Paid/free classification, quantity, event identity and gateway totals come from the route and server session rather than mutable hidden fields. The application rebuilds the quote from live ticket, variation, stock, early-bird, coupon and fee records before review and again before gateway dispatch; stale session prices and discounts have no authority. Coupons are event/date scoped and server calculated, malformed configuration fails closed, and a valid full discount becomes a zero-balance booking without a gateway charge. Per-option cumulative purchase limits are rechecked during quote review, locked stock reservation, free/offline booking creation and late-payment reacquisition. Authenticated buyers use customer identity; guests use a normalized keyed email fingerprint, including a migration backfill for historical guest purchases. The fingerprint uses a dedicated stable production secret rather than `APP_KEY`, so routine encryption-key rotation cannot silently reset historical limits. Its stored generation is versioned on guest bookings and reservations. A maintenance-only, dry-run-first rehash command audits recoverability and refuses partial mutation when a guest email is invalid or an active hold still depends on the old fingerprint; after those blockers are resolved it rehashes every historical guest booking atomically. Active guest and customer holds count toward the same cap, and final consumption must match the reservation buyer, so parallel sessions, token substitution or an expired payment returning after another purchase cannot oversubscribe the organizer's limit. Release readiness validates the complete reservation schema, dedicated key, current hash generation and live backfill/active-hold integrity before traffic. Replacing a payment hold is one ownership-bound transaction: the original remains active if the new selection fails, used replacement tokens cannot be replayed, and a temporary stock restore cannot leak a false capacity signal to the waitlist. Free checkout hashes a server-held reviewed-submission identity into a unique booking column and rechecks it under the locked event row, making double clicks and lost-response retries idempotent without consuming stock or issuing admission units twice. Its invoice and confirmation use the same encrypted, unique, monitored fulfilment queue as completed paid bookings. Sold-out limited tickets and variations now expose a consented waiting list on the event page. Contact data is encrypted with a one-way lookup hash; duplicate joins are idempotent; signed unsubscribe is self-service; and the scheduler invites people FIFO only up to currently available stock after accounting for unexpired invitations. Invitations explicitly do not reserve inventory, expire on a bounded window, are encrypted unique queue jobs with durable attempt/error state, and stuck/exhausted delivery uses the operational incident path. A released reservation triggers an immediate capacity check, the scheduled dispatcher catches every other inventory restoration path, and a matching completed booking closes the entry as converted. Event cancellation/closure suppresses dispatch and the release-readiness contract requires the waitlist schema.

Organizer guest-list and RSVP management now has a separate privacy-aware domain instead of reusing the buyer waiting list. An organizer can invite only for an owned, active scheduled event; guest names, email addresses and delivery errors are encrypted; normalized contact hashes make repeat invitations idempotent; and each invitation records a versioned organizer attestation that the organizer is permitted to contact the guest. RSVP links are expiring, signed and one-time-token protected. Essential RSVP processing consent is required only for acceptance, while optional marketing consent is captured separately. Organizers can review delivery/response state, cancel unused invitations and check in accepted guests under a row lock. Invitation jobs are encrypted, unique, retryable and operationally monitored; the database commit retains the current token only as encrypted hidden pending-dispatch state, a minutely poller reconstructs a lost queue handoff with the same job identity, and terminal delivery, response, cancellation, expiry or retention erases it. Jobs suppress delivery when the event is cancelled or deactivated, and approved cancellation invalidates outstanding guest invitations.

Bulk guest import and private export now follow the same privacy boundary. An organizer downloads a fixed three-column template, attests permission for every contact and uploads a bounded CSV to private storage. An encrypted, unique job revalidates each row through the invitation domain, rejects duplicate contacts within a file, makes successful rows import-idempotent and exposes row numbers plus safe correction messages without persisting source PII. Successful sources are deleted immediately; failed sources expire and are pruned daily. Guest CSV/XLSX exports are organizer/event scoped, require explicit operational-use confirmation, exclude anonymized records, capture a stable snapshot, neutralize spreadsheet formulas, run in the background and use the existing private actor-owned expiring-download contract. Dry-run-first retention irreversibly anonymizes only old terminal/expired contacts or accepted guests whose events ended, and refuses unresolved delivery states. The release gate requires the import and retention schema, private source storage and warns until the approved retention flag is enabled.

Still missing: policy approval to enable guest retention plus production mail-deliverability and RSVP usability drills. Checkout must also complete real-device and sandbox payment usability drills in production-like staging.

### Corporate registration

**MVP implemented:** an organizer-owned event mode switch separates corporate registration from ticket sales and prevents corporate events from entering ticket checkout. Organizers can create, version and publish a registration form; add, edit and reorder registrant- or attendee-scoped questions with drag-and-drop or accessible move controls; scope attendee questions to a registration/ticket type; use text, textarea, email, phone, number, date, select, radio, multi-select, checkbox and explicit consent fields; add semantic company, job-title, country and state fields plus a non-answer terms/information block; configure placeholders and server-enforced minimum/maximum character lengths; make questions required or conditionally visible; select automatic or manual approval; and review, approve or reject submissions with an auditable reason. Reordering is owner-scoped and transactional, returns a published form to draft, versions the definition, audits the before/after order and rejects any order that places a conditional controller after its dependent field. Editing follows the same owner and version boundaries, preserves historical answer snapshots, migrates dependent condition keys when a field key changes and rejects scope, ticket, type or option changes that would silently break an existing dependency. No-op edits remain idempotent. Conditional rules reject values outside a controlling select/radio/multi-select option set, require a meaningful comparison value, and normalize organizer-entered Yes/No checkbox and consent rules to the same booleans used by authoritative submission validation, preventing valid-looking rules that can never match. An optional organizer consent field persists an explicit encrypted Yes or No even when unchecked, is available to conditional logic, survives controlled CSV import and appears as a dedicated answer column in privacy-scoped CSV/XLSX exports rather than being conflated with mandatory platform processing consent. Reusable organizer-owned form templates retain versioned form metadata, questions and their presentation/validation settings; applying a template replaces the destination form definition, clears event-specific ticket assignments and returns a published form to draft for review. PDF/JPG/PNG file questions now use extension-to-detected-content agreement, bounded file/pixel and per-submission limits, active-PDF rejection and a private per-event object ledger. Original filenames are encrypted, answer snapshots expose no storage path, owner-only downloads are audited and non-cacheable, CSV imports fail clearly when a required upload cannot be supplied, exports identify only the private file reference, and retention removes the object before its answer metadata. Release readiness requires both the file ledger and a non-public upload disk. The public responsive flow captures a primary contact, repeatable attendees, each attendee's registration type when required, versioned consent and custom answers, then displays a non-sensitive reference and current approval status. Initial submitted/automatically-approved confirmation emails now repeat the structured registrant and attendee answers, represent consent as Yes/No, expose only a private upload's filename and cap pathological summaries so the operational mail remains deliverable. Contact values, attendee PII, answers and review notes are encrypted at rest; one-way contact hashes support safe lookup; submitted question/choice snapshots preserve historical meaning after the form changes; organizer ownership and published-form boundaries are enforced under transactions and row locks. Submission, approval, rejection and cancellation messages now use encrypted unique jobs with a durable per-registration delivery ledger, bounded retries, overlap protection, idempotent sends and stuck/exhausted incident monitoring. Approved event cancellation cancels active registrations and queues their cancellation notices, and the production readiness contract requires the complete registration, reusable-template, file and notification schema plus the event mode, ticket-scope and question-settings columns.

Bulk corporate registration is now implemented as a controlled import rather than a direct table upload. Organizers download a template derived from the current form, attest that they are authorized to register and contact each person, and upload a bounded CSV to private storage. Ticket-scoped forms add an `attendee_ticket_id` column so imported attendees are validated against the correct event registration type and only its applicable questions; events with variation-priced types also add `attendee_variation_name`, and the exact current option is required rather than inferred. Rows sharing a registration key become one multi-attendee submission; every value is revalidated through the published form; queued jobs are encrypted, unique, retryable and import-idempotent; row ledgers expose safe acceptance or rejection feedback without storing source PII; and completed source files are deleted immediately. Failed sources remain private only for a short retry window and the daily `registrations:prune-imports` task removes them at expiry. The release gate requires the import schema, source-link columns, attendee variation snapshot and private storage.

Corporate agendas are now first-class, organizer-owned records. Published sessions can be global or restricted to an event registration type, optional or required, scheduled only within the event window, located and capacity limited. Public and sponsor-allocated registration flows expose the same responsive agenda choices; imports accept pipe-separated `attendee_session_ids`; exports include selected session IDs and titles. Submission locks requested session rows in a stable order before checking capacity, so concurrent registrations cannot oversell a session. Overlapping attendee selections, mismatched registration types and missing required sessions fail validation. Rejecting a registration or cancelling its event releases/cancels active selections. A populated session cannot be silently cancelled or structurally changed; only a capacity increase is allowed without replacing the session and contacting attendees. The release gate requires both agenda tables.

The optional company invoice/PO path now binds approval to payment and ticket issue without treating registration as payment evidence. An organizer can issue one private PDF invoice for an approved submission only when every attendee has a supported registration type. Standard and variation-priced types are both supported: the first-party form and controlled CSV import require the attendee's exact option, persist it with the registration and reject stale or unknown values. Issuance groups attendees by ticket and option, snapshots each option's current unit price into a separate line item and reserves that exact variation inventory through the durable ticket-reservation ledger until a bounded due date. The PDF explicitly states that it is not a ticket. Recording payment requires a verified external reference and attestation; under row locks it consumes the reservation, creates one completed booking, records the gateway-scoped finance identity, updates earnings once, issues one signed ticket unit with the matching variation per attendee, binds each unit back to its attendee and queues the normal ticket delivery workflow. Duplicate retries are idempotent and a reused reference for another invoice fails closed. Overdue invoices expire every fifteen minutes and restore the same variation inventory; approved event cancellation cancels outstanding invoices. Badges and privacy-scoped exports carry the attendee's option snapshot so operations do not have to reconstruct it from mutable ticket configuration.

Ticketed corporate attendees now support organizer-designed, print-ready A6 badges. Organizers can set a heading and brand accent and map attendee-scoped form answers to company and role labels; mappings are constrained to the owned form rather than accepting arbitrary fields. Badge generation is owner-scoped, audited, private/no-store and bounded to 1,000 attendees per batch. Only issued or checked-in ticket units are printable, and every badge renders the existing signed ticket-unit QR used by the hardened check-in path instead of introducing a second credential. The transactional release gate requires the badge-settings schema.

Published corporate forms can now be embedded responsively on explicitly trusted websites. Organizers opt in per form, configure up to ten exact HTTPS origins (with HTTP permitted only for local development), select restrained accent/background colours and receive an iframe snippet with bounded automatic height updates. Wildcards, paths, credentials and insecure public origins are rejected. Embed responses remove the incompatible legacy frame header only for that route and enforce an exact `frame-ancestors` Content Security Policy even while the legacy site-wide CSP remains report-only. Submissions use a short-lived signed, throttled action rather than depending on third-party cookies for CSRF state; invalid signed submissions are rendered safely in place, and unsigned or expired posts fail closed. The isolated layout has no site navigation or footer, carries noindex metadata, and uses the same form validation, encryption, consent, capacity and notification domains as the first-party flow. Changes are ownership-scoped and audited, disabled embeds return 404, and release readiness requires the settings schema.

Sponsor-owned attendee allocations are now implemented without granting sponsors organizer access. An organizer reserves a bounded number of places for an attested sponsor contact; encrypted, unique queued mail delivers an expiring signed management portal. The sponsor must attest permission for each named contact, cannot exceed the allocation under a database lock, and each attendee receives a separate expiring signed link to complete their own answers and consent. The claim forces the invited identity, permits only one attendee, binds the resulting submission uniquely to the allocation claim and cannot be reused. Organizer cancellation or event cancellation invalidates outstanding links, delivery attempts are durable and monitored for stuck/exhausted failures, contact fields are encrypted with lookup-only hashes, and every material transition is audited. Registration retention also retires access tokens and anonymizes sponsor/attendee allocation contacts only after the allocation is terminal, its delivery is resolved and any completed linked submission has already been anonymized. The release gate requires both allocation tables, their privacy fields and the submission source-link column.

**Still missing beyond the MVP:** a signed-off privacy/consent policy. Retention automation now dry-runs by default, irreversibly anonymizes only old terminal records or approved/ticketed registrations whose events have ended, preserves operational history, refuses records with unresolved notification delivery, and is scheduled only after an approved environment flag enables it. Privacy-scoped corporate exports now produce one attendee per row with versioned registrant/attendee answers, registration type, agenda selections and authoritative admission evidence. Ticket-unit identity, point-in-time admission status, check-in timestamp and verified gate device are captured against the export-request cutoff, so later scans cannot leak into the requested snapshot. Requests remain organizer/event scoped, privacy-attested, encrypted, formula-injection safe, background generated, privately stored, signed for download, audited and automatically expired. The policy remains a governance/sign-off dependency and must not be represented as approved until legal/privacy review is recorded.

### Exports

Completed background exports now create one durable encrypted mail delivery for the requesting administrator, organizer, customer or scanner supervisor. The message links to the export's existing actor-owned signed download route, expires with the private file and still requires the same account to authenticate. Creation attempts and encrypted failures are recorded on the export, a minutely poller recovers missed notices and raises one privacy-safe stale incident, and the shared application-mail poller recovers a lost queue handoff without creating a second message. Export-history status remains the in-application notice; files are never attached to email or exposed through public storage.

**Done in this pass:** organizer and admin booking lists and reports queue the currently applied booking ID, event title, date, payment-method and payment-status filters to CSV, XLSX or PDF. Guest bookings are retained through left joins. Each request captures a stable maximum-booking and financial timestamp snapshot, stores encrypted filters, runs as an encrypted unique queue job, writes to a private disk, and records a durable actor-owned history entry. Booking rows now use the hardened sale ledger for gateway/reference/charged amount, aggregate only financially applied refunds at or before the snapshot, and attach only matched settlement ID/status/date/fee evidence checked before that cutoff. Formula-prefixed customer and event fields are neutralized. Downloads require both the authenticated requester and a short-lived signed URL. Files expire after the configured retention window, the scheduler deletes the private object while retaining audit/history, and retrying a completed job is idempotent. Empty and over-limit requests fail before queueing. The paginated report no longer serializes the entire dataset into the session.

Recurring booking exports are now implemented for administrators and organizers with encrypted filters, actor and organizer ownership, bounded rolling date windows, daily/weekly/monthly cadence, timezone-stable execution, scheduler locking, private expiring files, pause/resume controls, failure history and audit records. The existing row cap, snapshot, finance-evidence and download authorization controls are reused on every run; schedules never email attachments or create public links. Booking export columns are configurable per administrator role through a strict field allowlist. The selected policy is snapshotted into each encrypted request, applies equally to one-time and recurring files, defaults legacy configured roles to a minimal non-PII set, and cannot be bypassed by request fields; the primary administrator and owner-scoped organizer retain the full schema. Corporate attendee/form-answer exports use the same private, expiring, actor-owned delivery history as booking exports while preserving form-version snapshots, agenda selections, point-in-time ticket/check-in/gate evidence and excluding anonymized data. Release readiness rejects deployments missing recurring-export, role-policy or settlement schema.

The shop product-order report no longer serializes the selected result into the administrator session or performs a synchronous CSV-only download. The current date, payment-method, payment-status and order-status filters now create an encrypted point-in-time request for CSV or XLSX; an encrypted unique job writes one analysis-ready row per stable order ID to private storage, neutralizes spreadsheet formulas, uses plain numeric amount columns plus currency and ISO timestamps, excludes orders created after the request snapshot, records durable history and exposes only a signed, expiring, requesting-administrator download. Creating or downloading this money-bearing export requires both Shop Management scope and the Transaction permission.

Withdrawal tables now use the same durable evidence boundary for both finance administrators and organizers. Current reference, status and bounded date filters create an encrypted, point-in-time CSV/XLSX request; organizer requests are forcibly scoped to the requesting organizer, while administrator creation and download require both Withdraw Method and Transaction permissions. The encrypted unique job exports one row per stable withdrawal ID with plain amount/currency fields and ISO timestamps, masks the payout account to its last four digits, excludes full account numbers, provider recipient secrets, legacy arbitrary payout fields and internal decision reasons, and neutralizes spreadsheet formulas. Files remain private, actor-owned, expiring, audited and available only through short-lived signed `no-store` downloads.

The core transaction ledger now has a dedicated durable CSV/XLSX export for finance administrators and organizers instead of relying on the interactive table or a generic download route. Reference, transaction-type, payment-status and bounded date filters are encrypted with a stable maximum-ledger-ID snapshot; organizer requests are forcibly owner-scoped and administrator creation/download remains inside the Transaction permission boundary. The encrypted unique job emits one analysis-ready row per stable ledger ID with gateway and booking/order/customer/organizer identities, labelled transaction and payment states, plain gross/commission/tax/balance values and ISO timestamps. Historical booking/order identifiers are preserved as strings, formula-prefixed text is neutralized, empty or cross-organizer requests fail closed, completed retries are idempotent, and files remain private, actor-owned, expiring and available only through short-lived signed `no-store` downloads. Unrelated generic export routes reject this export type so a weaker permission boundary cannot be used to retrieve it.

Payment-reconciliation runs and bank-settlement summaries now expose the same durable finance-evidence workflow. Their current gateway, result-status and exact-date filters create encrypted CSV/XLSX requests bounded by a stable maximum source ID. The encrypted unique job exports stable run/settlement identities, row counts, reconciliation states, plain settlement totals and ISO timestamps while neutralizing spreadsheet formulas and deliberately excluding encrypted provider payloads and raw diagnostic errors. Empty requests fail before dispatch, later source rows cannot enter the logical snapshot, completed retries are idempotent, and the requesting Transaction-authorized administrator receives only a private, expiring, signed and `no-store` download. The interactive tables link to a shared actor-owned export history.

The row-level payment and settlement reconciliation detail tables use the same export boundary rather than forcing finance to rely on screenshots or copy/paste. Each request is bound to one authoritative run or settlement, preserves the selected normalized result classification and captures a stable maximum-entry ID. Files carry joinable entry, run/settlement, payment-intent and booking IDs; formula-safe gateway references; plain expected, charged, settled and fee amounts; currencies; local/provider states; and ISO check timestamps. Encrypted detail payloads—including provider object IDs and internal ledger diagnostics—are excluded at the database query, while empty or cross-parent result sets fail before dispatch. Detail exports share the requesting administrator's private signed expiring history and idempotent encrypted job.

Operational-incident and webhook-delivery tables now have dedicated private CSV/XLSX evidence exports under the Transaction permission boundary. Incident status/severity and webhook gateway/outcome/bounded date filters are encrypted with a stable maximum source ID. Strict database column allowlists prevent encrypted incident context, incident key hashes, resolution notes and webhook reference hashes from entering the export query at all; the generated files retain stable public identities, operational states, delivery/signature evidence, bounded error codes, HTTP results, durations and ISO timestamps. Spreadsheet formulas are neutralized, webhook windows are limited to 31 days, empty or oversized requests fail before dispatch, later records remain outside the membership snapshot, retries are idempotent, and only the requesting administrator can use the signed expiring `no-store` download history.
